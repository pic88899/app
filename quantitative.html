Quantitative Trading refers to an investment method that uses computer technology and mathematical models to automatically execute trading strategies by analyzing a large amount of historical data. It is different from traditional subjective trading, but relies on data and models to make investment decisions, thereby reducing the impact of human emotional fluctuations. Common strategies for quantitative trading include: Statistical arbitrage: using statistical models to find price differences between different markets and make profits by buying and selling two assets at the same time. Trend following: identifying market trends and trading, such as buying when prices rise and selling when prices fall. Mean reversion: assuming that asset prices will return to the average level, and when prices deviate from the average level, reverse trading is performed. High-frequency trading: a large number of transactions are made in a very short period of time to capture small price fluctuations. Event-driven: trading based on specific events (such as company performance releases, policy adjustments). The advantages of quantitative trading are: Reduce emotional impact: eliminate personal emotions from trading The impact of decision-making, more objective investment.﻿ Improve efficiency: Use computer technology to achieve automatic trading, improve transaction efficiency and speed.﻿ Reduce costs: Large amounts of data can be processed in batches to reduce transaction costs.﻿ Enhance data analysis capabilities: Potential investment opportunities can be discovered through data mining, such as discovering the small price difference between the buying price and the selling price of a certain security.﻿ The disadvantages of quantitative trading are:﻿ Requires professional knowledge: A certain foundation in mathematics, statistics and programming is required. Model risk: Improper model establishment may lead to transaction failure. Technical risk: System failure may cause transaction delays or errors. Market risk: Market fluctuations may lead to losses.
